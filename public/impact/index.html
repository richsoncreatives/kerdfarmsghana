Kerd Farms Egg & Nutrition Outreach

One Egg. A Healthier Beginning.

Every child deserves the opportunity to grow, learn and thrive. Through community outreach, school visits and nutrition education, Kerd Farms seeks to make nutritious food more accessible while inspiring families and communities to prioritise children's wellbeing.

Why this matters

Agriculture should do more than produce food.

A child who is not eating enough of the right food finds it harder to concentrate, harder to grow, and harder to stay well. In the communities we work in, the gap is often not knowledge but access — and the two are addressed together or not at all.

Protein matters particularly in the years when a child is growing quickly. Eggs are one accessible source of high-quality protein and other nutrients, which is why we use them as the centrepiece of our outreach rather than as a novelty.

A single visit does not change a child’s diet. Nutrition education for pupils, parents and caregivers is what carries the message past the day of the outreach.

Our aspiration, stated plainly

We want to encourage access to protein-containing foods at mealtimes — according to what is available locally, affordable, suitable for the individual child and consistent with family circumstances. Eggs can form part of a varied, balanced diet; no single food supplies everything a child needs, and we do not suggest that every child must eat an egg at every meal.

The outreach work runs under the working name Kerd Farms Egg & Nutrition Outreach, with the internal “Good Protein” identity. Names and programme structure are confirmed by the business before publication.

Why eggs can be part of the solution

A practical source of protein, with real caveats.

Protein and nutrients

A single hen’s egg provides roughly six grams of protein along with a range of vitamins and minerals. It is one of the more affordable animal-source proteins in most Ghanaian markets.

Affordability and availability

Protein-rich foods are the hardest part of a healthy diet for many households to afford consistently, which is why working with the food that is already produced locally matters.

Food safety comes first

Eggs must be handled, cooked and stored properly. Children with an egg allergy must not be given them, and individual dietary needs always take precedence over a general recommendation.

Nutritional figures are approximate and drawn from published food composition data (see sources below). Dietary diversity, allergies, food safety and what a family can actually afford all have to be considered alongside protein content.

Programme activities

What the programme does, and what we intend to do.

The activities below describe the programme’s scope. They are plans and activities undertaken by the programme, not a claim about how many children or schools have been reached — those figures are published only once verified.

Free boiled-egg and pepper distribution

Provided during school visits and community outreach activities, prepared and transported under hygienic conditions.

Child-friendly nutrition education

Age-appropriate sessions that help children understand why a varied diet matters, delivered in language and terms they can use.

School engagement and community sensitisation

Working with teachers and community leaders so the message is reinforced beyond a single visit.

Parent and caregiver education

Practical guidance for households on including protein-containing foods in daily meals within their circumstances.

Sponsorships and product donations

Support from businesses and organisations to extend how many schools and children the programme can reach.

Nutrition awareness campaigns

Community events and communication that keep children’s nutrition on the local agenda.

Food safety & child safeguarding

Non-negotiable requirements.

Any food we distribute to children must be safe, and any child involved in our work must be protected. These requirements are not optional.

Preparation and handling

Eggs must be fully boiled, prepared in clean conditions, transported safely and kept at a suitable temperature until served.

Allergies and individual needs

Children with a known egg allergy must not be given eggs. Supervision by responsible adults is required at every distribution.

Accompaniments

Pepper and any other accompaniment must be appropriate for the age group and individual dietary needs. Not every child can safely eat the same foods.

Consent and photographs

School visits and photography require the permissions of the school and of parents or guardians. We do not publish identifiable photographs or personal details of children without those permissions.
